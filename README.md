# CS 250 Sprint Review and Retrospective
## Interpreting User Needs
I interpret user needs by listening to the client and turning what they say into clear user stories. A user story follows the format "As a user, I want this so that I get this benefit." This keeps the focus on what the user actually needs instead of what the developer assumes. User stories also include acceptance criteria, so the team knows exactly when a feature is done and working the way the user expects.
## My Approach to Developing Programs
I approach development in small pieces instead of trying to build everything at once. Working in sprints lets me finish one feature, get feedback, and adjust before moving on. In the future I want to keep using Agile processes like daily stand ups, sprint planning, backlog refinement, and retrospectives. Retrospectives especially help because the team looks at what went well and what to improve before the next sprint.
## Being a Good Team Member
Being a good team member means communicating openly, sharing updates in stand ups, and asking for help early when something is blocked. It also means respecting each role on the Scrum team, from the Product Owner to the testers, and being open to feedback. A good team member focuses on the team's goal, not just their own tasks.
